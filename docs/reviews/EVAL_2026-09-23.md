## 0. Scope, sources and how to read this

**The owner's framing (binding).** The primary question of the programme is: *which kinds of information that a
controller could use can be read out of a backbone's internal geometry, how reliably, and does the geometry add anything
beyond what the output head (softmax / logits) already gives?* The label-free adapt / hold / escalate controller, and
then a robot, are the **final** goal, not the current one. The map-first work is judged first on the primary question,
and the final goal is graded separately.

**The owner's principles (binding).**
1. Prior art is not a reason to drop or down-rank anything. It is a starting point to adopt and refine toward Atlas's
   direction.
2. What matters is end functionality, not novelty. Every item is judged by whether the controller can act on it
   (adapt / hold / escalate; F1 suspicious-input flagging; F2 information-gated feeding), how reliably (AUROC, false
   alarms, detection delay, cost), and whether it beats or complements the cheapest existing baseline (logit gap,
   max-softmax, a simple density).

So this evaluation gives **no novelty grade**. In its place it grades **functional usefulness** and **use of prior art**
(baselines and starting points), for both the primary and the final goal.

**Sources.**

  science-and-novelty (C; its novelty grade is not carried over, see 2.5), end-goal-relevance (C),

## 1. Bottom line

**On the primary question.**
1. **Breadth probed: wide.** About 45 kinds of geometry → information were probed across six families (per-sample
   confidence; density/OOD; harm; shift type and TTA; collapse, dimension and similarity; probing and corruption
   geometry). About 25 received a pre-registered verdict, and the negatives are recorded.
2. **What geometry reliably yields, within its scope** (CIFAR-10 ResNet20/56, one recipe, CIFAR-10-C; plus three
   supervised ImageNet models for the margin only):
   - a per-sample confident-mistake ranking at the head input (CM-1, CM-3);
   - a split- and batch-level shift magnitude that tracks accuracy loss, with a conservative HOLD band (LH-1, LH-2);
   - severity-graded familiarity for non-noise corruptions (DO-1);
   - a law that predicts a density detector's clean false-alarm rate from one collapse number (DO-3);
   - seed-stable checkpoint descriptors (ID profile, nc1, valley depth, class adjacency at depth 20, commit layer),
     whose shapes carry from depth 20 to 56 while their levels depend on depth and fit (CD-1 to CD-8, PC-3);
   - clean negatives: TwoNN is the PCA spectrum (CD-2); energy flips sign across datasets (CM-8) and fails as a hold
     rule (LH-3); the off-simplex residual (ST-4), the class-orthogonal drift detector (ST-3) and class_sub_frac (PC-6)
     carry nothing usable; earlier layers give no early warning of confident mistakes (CM-9).
3. **Does geometry add beyond the output head? Answered for one family only, on clean data.**
   - resnet20: margin = maxprob.
   - Full-fit resnet56: margin beats maxprob by +0.006 / +0.012 / +0.020 (p 0.027 / 0.0017 / 0.0004); not a tie
     artifact. The logit gap was never computed on CIFAR (it is recomputable).
   - ResNet50: the head's logit gap beats margin (−0.049 / −0.053 on all errors; −0.046 / −0.047 at matched
     confidence).
   - Two DeiT-lineage ViT-B/16: margin ≈ logit gap on all errors, but **ahead at matched confidence in all four runs at
     every cut 0.5-0.8** (+0.008 to +0.037, p ≤ 0.047; V3b, pre-registered, predicted EQUIVALENT). This is the only
     pre-registered beyond-logit positive in the programme (CM-5).
   - Never compared with a head statistic: the harm grade H/h, density familiarity, OOD sparsity, the family router,
     class adjacency, cross-model agreement, nearest-centre/head disagreement, and every signal under shift.
4. **Net answer.** Geometry yields a usable shift-magnitude axis and several calibration facts, but none of them has yet
   been shown to beat free output-based monitors. The per-sample axis mostly restates head confidence, with one small
   conditional lead on two related ViTs.

**Reading.**
- Where the comparison was run it was designed well; B1 named the logit gap "the controller-relevant comparison"
  (results/margin_b1_vitb16/SESSION.md:517).
- It covers one of six families, and only clean data.
- The largest gap is the comparison of the shift-level signals (H, h, density, router) with free output monitors. The
  literature's expectation is unfavourable for geometry on synthetic corruptions: feature-distribution distances look
  good there but transfer poorly across shift types, and confidence-based estimators are the baseline
  (`docs/knowledge/label-free-accuracy-harm.md` §4).
- **Between-split rank correlation cannot answer the question.** In AX-3 about 99% of the per-batch loss variance lies
  between splits (within-split SD about 2.1 pt; between-split SD about 20.5 pt), so any statistic monotone in shift
  size scores near 1. Atlas's own rule-8 check found that displacement magnitude (0.9996 / 0.995) and sparse_frac
  (0.994 / 0.992) match H (results/anomaly_h1/SESSION.md:375-378).
- The comparison is cheap: `preds/<split>.npz` hold maxprob for every CIFAR split, the CIFAR logits are recomputable,
  and the ImageNet `preds` carry the logit gap.
- In the ViT head (768 → 1000), the head is injective on its input if its weights have full column rank, so at that tap
  geometry can at most be a better *statistic* of what the logits hold. Information the head *discards* can exist only
  where the head is lossy (the CIFAR 64 → 10 head) or at earlier taps
  (`docs/reviews/EXTRACTION_PROPOSALS_2026-09-23.md`, X2).

**Verdict on beyond-head evidence: D+ / C-.** Answered for one family, on clean data: mostly NO, with one pre-registered
conditional positive on two DeiT-lineage ViTs. Everything at batch, shift, OOD and similarity level is untested. This is
the weakest element of the primary question and the cheapest to fix.

### 2.4 Scope and generality

### 2.5 Use of prior art (baselines and starting points)

Per the owner's principles, prior art is graded as a starting point that Atlas should adopt and refine, not as a
novelty penalty. The question here is how well Atlas used it. The adoption and refinement plan is
`docs/knowledge/README.md` §8.

- **Instruments were adopted and refined well.** Rows 1, 2 and 8 were built as known-answer measurements, and the code
  cites their sources: atlas/invariants/dimension.py:5-9 (Facco 2017; Ansuini 2019, "Known shape to sanity-check
  against"), landmarks.py:6-7 (Papyan, Han & Donoho 2020), compare.py:18 (Moschella 2023), compare.py:205 (Geirhos
  2020). Atlas added refinements: reference-draw twins, random-init nulls for every row, and a Gaussian spectral twin
  for TwoNN at every tap (CD-2). The last two extend controls that Ansuini et al. 2019 ran on untrained networks and at
  one layer (their Fig. 5B-C).
- **One head baseline was adopted well, and it produced the key result.** B1 pre-registered the head's logit gap as "the
  controller-relevant comparison" (results/margin_b1_vitb16/SESSION.md:517; the H2b rule, ATLAS_STATUS.md:20). That
  choice is why CM-5 (V3b) is the only beyond-logit evidence in the programme.
- **The free standard baselines were mostly not adopted.** No logit-based head statistic on CIFAR (the logit gap is
  recomputable); no AC, DoC or ATC next to the harm grade; no MSP, energy or Mahalanobis next to the OOD flag rates; no
  BBSE / BBSDh for label shift; no CUSUM or sequential false-alarm control; no Trust Score next to the margin, although
  it is the direct precedent (Jiang, Kim, Guan & Gupta, NeurIPS 2018) and reported the same CIFAR result A3 found. The
  MSP baseline (Hendrycks & Gimpel 2017) and the finding that MSP is often not the best head statistic (Cattelan & Silva
  2024) bear directly on rows 9 and 10.
- **Known refinements of adopted methods were not applied.** `knn_density` is deep kNN (Sun et al. 2022) without its L2
  normalisation and with a train-referenced threshold instead of held-out calibration (atlas/invariants/density.py:18-53),
  which is the source of the DO-3 over-alarm. Probe pools lack control tasks and grouped folds
  (`docs/knowledge/probing-corruption-geometry.md` §3).
- **Prior art is not cited where decisions are recorded.** ATLAS_STATUS.md, the plans and the SESSION files cite none of
  the above; He & Su's law of equi-separation (the Stage 2 F2 finding), Kornblith's CKA and Hui et al. are cited nowhere
  in the tracked repo.
- **What prior art does not cover** is listed factually, with scope, in `docs/knowledge/README.md` §9 (CM-5, the DO-3
  law, the CM-2 collapse curve, the CM-8 sign flip, the CD-2 twin result, CD-3). Its functional value is in the readiness
  column of the inventory: CM-5 is a candidate, DO-3 a usable calibration rule, the rest are negatives or descriptors.
- **Corrections from the knowledge research to the audits' own prior-art notes.** Sun et al. 2022 (deep kNN) contains no
  corruption or severity experiment, so "kNN sparsity rising with severity" is not attributable to it; the held-out
  calibration requirement comes from OpenOOD v1.5, DkNN and split-conformal testing, not from Sun et al.
  (`docs/knowledge/density-ood.md` §4).

**Verdict on use of prior art: C-.** Instruments were adopted, cited in code and refined well; the free standard
baselines and the known refinements of adopted detectors were mostly not used, which is exactly why the beyond-head
question is still open. It is the cheapest grade to raise: every baseline in `docs/knowledge/README.md` §8 runs on
existing dumps.

## 3. The final goal: the label-free controller

**What a controller could use today** (all on clean data or depth-56 CIFAR-10-C):
1. **Per-sample trust: the head's own best statistic,** chosen offline per checkpoint (maxprob or a logit-based score on
   CIFAR; the logit gap on the ViTs and ResNet50). On the ViTs the margin carries residual error information beyond
   maxprob that the logit gap lacks at matched confidence (V3b); on ResNet50 it does not; on CIFAR it is unresolved
   (margin beats maxprob in resnet56, E9 p ≤ 0.027, and the logit gap was not measured). A joint detector is untested.
2. **A batch harm grade h with a HOLD band** (AX-3, SUPPORTED at depth 56). At batch 256 and h > 0.25:

   | batch loss | s1 flagged | s2 flagged |
   |---|---|---|
   | > 10 pt (harmful) | 3350/3351 | 3253/3254 |
   | 5-10 pt | 912/1091 | 931/1026 |
   | ≤ 10 pt (all non-harmful) | 1099/2649 | 1198/2746 |
   | ≤ 2 pt (benign) | 13/1050 | 26/1161 |
   | clean | 0/200 | 0/200 |

   Limits: effective n is about 30 splits per seed; within-split Spearman is only 0.21-0.23; batches overlap (200 per
   split from 2,000 rows); never compared with batch-mean maxprob, entropy, ATC or DoC; no stream or sequential
   false-alarm control; it predicts harm, not whether adapting would help.
3. **Calibration rules and negatives:** calibrate density thresholds on held-out deployment-clean data (DO-3); drop
   energy/DEVIATION from any hold rule (LH-3); do not use TwoNN (CD-2); brightness is low-harm at depth-56 CIFAR (LH-4).

**What it lacks:**
- **Closed-loop evidence.** scripts/tta_deform.py and experiments/queue/tta_tent_resnet20_{fog3,collapse}.yaml date
  from day 1 and were never run. The TTA ladder was sequenced after B1 (AGENT_LOOP.md:108), which closed at HEAD, so
  "never run" is a sequencing fact, not a violation. Its planned bar is lenient toward geometry (unequal onset rules,

- **An adaptation-benefit signal.** Nothing measures whether adapting on a batch helps, which is the actual adapt/hold

- **A controller specification.** MASTER's P5 was never written (MASTER_SUMMARY.md:266-269).
- **Streaming realism, Gate 3, self-supervised or robot encoders, latency and memory** of kNN-to-reference.

**HOLD is one action with two triggers.** In both ANOMALY_H1.md:308-310 and MASTER_SUMMARY.md:269, HOLD means "do not
adapt". The triggers differ: a harmless shift ("hold, output trusted") and an untrustworthy input ("hold, output
distrusted"). The literature adds two distinct actions a controller spec must name separately: roll back / reset a
diverging adaptation, and escalate when a risk bound is violated (`docs/knowledge/shift-type-tta-monitoring.md` §7).

**Functional usefulness by controller function.** Sub-variants follow
`docs/knowledge/controller-functions-feasibility.md` §0; the per-function evidence and gaps are in

## 5. Key weaknesses and overstatements

### 5.1 Against the primary question

1. **The beyond-head question was answered for one family only, and only on clean data** (2.3). The ANOMALY_H1 probe
   excluded head statistics by design: it reads only argmax (scripts/anomaly_probe.py:302) and refuses logits
   (docs/plans/ANOMALY_H1.md:585-588). So H, h, the router and density were never set against their free head peers.
2. **The CIFAR logit gap was never computed.** It is recomputable from the dumped float16 penult and the fc weights, or by
   re-extracting 5,000 images; `preds` store only argmax and maxprob (atlas/extract_acts.py:188).
3. **"Beats maxprob" was at risk of being read as "beats the head".** Maxprob is saturated on CIFAR and broken by
   soft-label recipes on ResNet50. Atlas's own H2b rule guards against this (ATLAS_STATUS.md:20), but row 9's M56-c and
   E9 results are easy to over-read.
4. **Positives selected by the baseline's own score.** Type-b is selected on maxprob and scored against all correct
   samples, which builds maxprob's weakness into the test; ResNet50's V1 reversal is this selection artifact
   (results/margin_b1_vitb16/SESSION.md:489-495). All-errors and risk-coverage endpoints were never primary.
5. **Between-split rank correlation was used as the evidence for harm grading,** a test any monotone shift statistic
   passes (2.3).
6. **Narrow scope** (2.4), **spent depth-56 seeds**, the **corruption-axis holdout leak**, **mixed probe pools** and
   **GAP-only pooling**.
7. **Prior art not used as a starting point** (2.5): known results are presented without citation in the status and
   SESSION files, and the published baselines and refinements for the adopted detectors (L2 normalisation, held-out
   calibration, Trust Score, ATC / DoC, BBSE) were not run.
